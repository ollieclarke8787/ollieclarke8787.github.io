    Boop is played on a 6×6 bed. You each start with eight kittens in hand, and you win by getting three of your cats in a row. Orange places first.

    A turn

    Place one piece from your hand on any empty square. That is the whole turn: pieces already on the bed are never picked up and moved, they only ever get pushed around by what you place.

    Booping

    The piece you place pushes — boops — every piece in the eight squares around it one square directly away from itself. Yours and your opponent's alike.

    • A piece only moves if the square it would land on is empty. If anything is already there, it stays where it is.
    • A piece booped off the edge of the bed goes back to its owner's hand.
    • Kittens cannot boop cats. A kitten you place shoves the kittens around it and leaves the cats standing. A cat boops everything.

    Every direction is worked out from the square you played, so a piece can be booped at most once per turn, and no boop can open a gap for another.

    Graduating

    Once the booping has settled, look for three of your pieces in a row — along a rank, a file or a diagonal, on adjacent squares. All three come off the bed and go back into your hand as cats, whether they were kittens, cats or a mix of the two.

    It does not matter how the line came about: the piece you placed need not be in it, and a row you shunt your own pieces into by booping counts just the same. Only the player who has just placed can graduate, and if there is a line, graduating is compulsory. Make two lines at once and you choose which one goes.

    Winning

    • Three of your cats in a row wins on the spot. The line is not graduated — the game is simply over.
    • Getting all eight of your pieces onto the bed as cats also wins.

    When your hand is empty

    If all eight of your pieces are on the bed you have nothing left to place, so your turn is a different thing: take one of your own pieces off the bed, graduate it, and put it straight back down as a cat on any empty square — including the one it has just left. It boops from there like any other placement, and can complete a line like any other.

    Where this board differs from the printed rules

    Two known gaps, shared with the engine this board takes its rules from:

    • A line you shove your opponent's pieces into is not spotted. Only the mover's own pieces are ever checked.
    • The empty-hand turn here lets you graduate a cat as well as a kitten; the printed rules allow only a kitten.

    This is an analysis board, not an opponent. Nothing here plays against you: you play both sides, branch wherever you like, and step back and forth through what you have built.

    Playing a move

    • Click an empty square to place a piece there.
    • If you hold both a kitten and a cat, a small picker appears in the colour of the side to move. Otherwise the only piece you have goes straight down.
    • A placement that completes one line graduates it for you. One that completes two stops and lists them; hover an option to see that line lit up on the board, and click to take it.

    The empty-hand turn

    When every one of your pieces is on the bed, the board asks in the order the move happens:

    1. Your pieces are ringed. Click the one to graduate — it takes a heavier ring.
    2. Every square the cat may go to is dotted, the square it came from included. Click one to play the move.

    Clicking a different piece of yours picks that one instead, and Esc clears the selection.

    Moving through the line

    Click any move in the list to jump to it, use the left and right arrow keys, or scroll the wheel over the board — up steps back, down steps forward. The move you are on is highlighted, and the list keeps it in view.

    Variations

    Play a move that differs from the one already recorded and the line branches. Right-click a move — or use the buttons under the board — for:

    • Promote variation — move that line up one level among its siblings.
    • Make main line — make it the principal line, demoting the rest by one and keeping their order.
    • Delete from here — remove the line from that move onward.

    All three act on the point where the line diverges rather than on the move you happen to be looking at, so promoting from deep inside a variation does what you mean.

    Position

    The FEN box holds the position you are looking at. Copy it out, or paste one in and press Load FEN to start a fresh line from it.

    Move list

    The Move list box holds the line as text. It shows the main line — whichever move comes first at each branch, which is what make main line decides. Tick include variations to add the branches in brackets, PGN-style: 

    1. Kc3 Kd4 2. Kc4 (2. Kb1 Kf6) (2. Ke5)

    Load moves reads that back, variations and all, so exported text pastes straight in and every branch stays clickable. A bracketed group is an alternative to the move just played, so it branches from the position before it.

    Notation

    Squares are named like a chessboard: files a–f left to right, ranks 1–6 bottom to top, so a1 is the bottom-left corner.

    Kc3place a kitten on c3
    Cc3place a cat on c3
    a1>Ce3hand empty: a1 graduates and is placed, as a cat, on e3
    Kc1=a1b1c1the placement completed that line, and it graduates
    a1>Ce3=c1d2e3both at once
    Kc1=a1b1c1#and it won the game

    = echoes chess promotion, which is what graduation is. > reads as "becomes", written source-first because the piece leaves a1 before the cat lands on e3. Every token says exactly what happened, so a game replays from its move list alone.

    The board editor

    Board editor, beside New game under the board, sets up any position. Five tiles appear beside the board — a kitten and a cat for each side, a cross that removes pieces, and a bin that clears the whole board. Click a tile to pick it up, then click squares to place that piece over and over.

    • Drag across squares to fill a run of them in one stroke — erasing too, if you started on a square that already held the selected piece.
    • Click a square that already holds the selected piece and it comes off again, so a tile is its own undo. A different piece is simply replaced.
    • Right-click, over the board or over the tiles, to swap to the other player's piece of the same kind — laying out both sides never needs a trip back to the tiles.

    Choose whose turn it is and how many kittens and cats each player holds. Every player owns exactly eight pieces counting the bed and the hand together, so a total that does not add up keeps Continue from here disabled until you fix it. Continuing leaves the editor and starts a fresh line from that position.

    A line that starts anywhere but the opening position carries a tag at the top of the move list saying where, and the tag is written into the exported text and read back on import: 

    [FEN "6/4B1/3a2/2a3/6/6-60-70-bp"] 1... Ka1 2. Kf6

    Either side can move first from such a position. When grey begins, the first move is numbered 1... rather than 1., as a chess game continued from black's turn would be.
